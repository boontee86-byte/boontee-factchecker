The single most significant structural advantage for Singapore-based equity investors is the absence of capital gains tax on appreciation of securities, including US stocks.

## The Compounding Advantage

Consider a US investor who buys a stock at $50 and sells at $1,000. That investor owes capital gains tax on the $950 of appreciation. A Singaporean investor holding the same position owes nothing on the gain.

Over the long run, this difference is enormous. Compounding works best when gains are not periodically harvested by the tax authority. Every dollar that stays invested continues to generate returns, and those returns generate further returns. The absence of capital gains tax means the full value of appreciation remains in the portfolio, working for the investor.

Singapore deliberately does not tax capital gains as a matter of national policy. This is a structural feature of the tax system, not an oversight or temporary measure. The government's revenue model relies on other sources, including GST and income tax, rather than taxing investment returns.

## After-Tax Return Comparison

The after-tax return from long-term equity appreciation is structurally superior in Singapore compared to jurisdictions that impose capital gains tax. For investors with a multi-decade time horizon, the difference compounds into a substantial gap in terminal wealth.

Singapore-listed stocks also pay dividends without withholding tax for local investors. This means both capital appreciation and domestic dividend income flow to the investor without tax friction.

## Practical Implications

- Long-term buy-and-hold strategies are especially rewarded in Singapore's tax environment
- Portfolio rebalancing through selling does not trigger a tax event on gains
- The incentive to defer selling for tax reasons (as exists in the US) does not apply, allowing cleaner portfolio management decisions

The zero capital gains tax environment is the foundation upon which the rest of a Singapore-based investment strategy should be built.
