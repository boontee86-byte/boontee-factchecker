Leverage Elimination

Why leverage converts temporary drawdowns into permanent losses and why eliminating it is a prerequisite for holding through volatility.

Leverage is the mechanism that converts a temporary drawdown into a permanent loss. When you borrow to invest, a large enough decline forces you to sell — not because you changed your mind, but because you ran out of room. Without leverage, you always retain the ability to choose. That choice is everything.

Margin Calls Remove Agency

When a leveraged position declines past the maintenance threshold, the broker liquidates your holdings automatically. The decision to hold or sell is taken from you. This is the core problem: the single greatest advantage of a long-term investor — patience — is destroyed by leverage.

Even if the thesis is right, forced selling at the wrong time crystallizes losses that would have recovered. You can be correct about the destination and still lose everything because leverage removed your ability to survive the journey.

The Only Scenario That Works

The only scenario where holding through volatility is possible is when no one can make you sell. This requires:

  • No margin debt — No borrowed money in the brokerage account.
  • No leveraged instruments — No products that amplify exposure beyond 1x.
  • No obligations tied to portfolio value — No loans or commitments that trigger based on account balance.

If your portfolio dropped 50% tomorrow, would any entity — broker, lender, or counterparty — force you to take action? If the answer is yes, you have leverage exposure that needs to be eliminated before you can claim to be a long-term investor.

Leverage Feels Productive in Rising Markets

The temptation of leverage is that it works spectacularly in rising markets. A 2x leveraged position doubles the gains. But this symmetry works in reverse, and markets do not rise in a straight line. The question is not whether leverage improves returns in good years — it does. The question is whether you survive the bad years. Unleveraged investors always survive.
